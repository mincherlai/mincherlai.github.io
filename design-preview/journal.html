About the Laboratory

The laboratory studies how atomic-scale defects and lattice dynamics decide what a halide perovskite can actually do — in a solar cell, a sensor, or a memory device. The work begins at surfaces and interfaces, where the material meets the world and where most of its behaviour is set.

Photoelectron spectroscopy with synchrotron radiation reads the electronic structure; terahertz time-domain spectroscopy reads the lattice. Put together, the two give a quantitative account of defect states and the phonon modes that carry carriers through the soft lattice.

The laboratory is led by Min-Cherl Jung, Associate Professor (Research) at the Global Institute of Future Technology, Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

Research Themes

Surfaces & Interfaces

Electronic structure of perovskite surfaces and heterointerfaces, measured by PES, XPS, ARPES and NEXAFS at synchrotron beamlines.

Defects, Quantified

Defect species, states and densities measured quantitatively — and correlated with terahertz absorption on the same films.

Lattice Dynamics

Phonon modes, polaron formation and carrier dynamics in a soft, anharmonic lattice, read by THz-TDS and ultrafast spectroscopy.

Devices

Solar cells, THz sensors and neuromorphic memory elements built on the same quantitative understanding of the thin film.
